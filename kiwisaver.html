How KiwiSaver works in real life

KiwiSaver is New Zealand's savings scheme for retirement. These are the real rules from 1 April 2026, and this game follows them.

  • Joining. Starting a new job signs you up automatically. You can opt out between your 2nd and 8th week, and you get back what you put in. After that you stay a member, but you can take a break, called a "savings suspension".
  • You. 3.5% of your pay before tax goes in every pay day, unless you choose 4%, 6%, 8% or 10%. If 3.5% is too much, you can ask to drop to 3% for a while.
  • Your employer. They add 3.5% of your pay on top, or 3% if you're on 3%. That money is taxed first. This is called ESCT (Employer Superannuation Contribution Tax), and the rate depends on how much you earn in a year: 10.5% up to $18,720, 17.5% up to $64,200, 30% up to $93,720, 33% up to $216,000, and 39% above that.
  • The government. They add 25 cents for every $1 you put in, up to $260.72 a year (1 July to 30 June). To get the full amount, you need to put in $1,042.86 in a year. If you earn over $180,000, you get nothing. In real life it's paid once a year. In this game it's added every pay day.
  • Funds. Your money is invested. Conservative funds are steady, and growth funds go up more over time but drop more often. New members start in a Balanced fund. Funds also charge small fees, which this game leaves out.
  • Getting it out. The money is locked until you're 65. You can get it out early to buy your first home (after 3 years, leaving $1,000 in), in significant financial hardship (not the government's money), if you're seriously ill, or if you move overseas for good (not to Australia).

Sources: Inland Revenue (ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver) and the KiwiSaver changes from Budget 2025.
