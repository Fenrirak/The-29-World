Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 2026

The 29 World is a classroom economy simulator that teachers use to run an in-class currency, bank, jobs, and marketplace for their students. This page explains what information the app collects, how it's used, and who can see it.

1. Who this applies to

Two kinds of accounts use this app: teachers, who create a class and set its rules, and students, who join a specific class using a class code the teacher gives them. Everything below applies to both, except where noted.

2. Information we collect

We only collect what's actually needed to run the classroom simulation, and nothing more. Providing a username, display name, and password is necessary to create and use an account — without them, the app can't work — but beyond that, the only other thing we ask for is an optional recovery email from teachers, described below.

Account information. When you create an account, we store the username, display name, and role (teacher or student) you provide, along with the class you belong to. Teachers can also choose a title (such as Ms or Mr) and a last name, which is used so students see their teacher addressed that way. We do not ask for or collect a phone number, date of birth, or home address from anyone, and we never ask students for an email address. Behind the scenes, the login system requires an email-shaped identifier to work, so the app generates a placeholder one from your username automatically — it isn't a working address and nothing is ever sent to it.

Optional teacher recovery email. Teachers can choose to add an email address when they sign up, or later from Teacher Home, so they can get back into their account if they forget their password. It is optional, only teachers can add one, and it can be changed or removed at any time from Teacher Home (removing it also removes the fingerprint described below). The address is kept in a separate record that only that teacher (and the developer, see Section 4) can read; students and classmates cannot see it. To check a username and email pair before sending a reset email, the app also stores a one-way fingerprint (a hash) of the username and email. This fingerprint can be looked up by username without signing in, but it does not contain the email address and the address can't be read out of it, although someone who already knows a username could test guesses of the email against it. When a teacher who has forgotten their password enters their username and recovery email, and the pair matches, Google Firebase sends a one-time sign-in link to that address. Opening the link lets the teacher choose a new password, and from then on that teacher signs in with their recovery email and the new password instead of their username. If the pair doesn't match, no email is sent, and the screen looks the same either way.

Password. Your password is handled by Google Firebase Authentication and is not stored by this app in readable form. When a teacher creates a batch of student accounts, the passwords are generated in the teacher's browser, shown to the teacher once, and are not saved by this app; it's up to the teacher to hand them out and keep any printed or downloaded list safe.

Failed login attempts. To protect accounts from password guessing, we keep a small counter for each username recording how many wrong passwords have recently been entered and when. After too many wrong attempts the account is locked for 30 minutes. This record contains no IP address or device details, and it resets after a successful login or once the lockout period has passed.

In-app simulation data. As you use the app, it stores the data needed to run the simulation: your play-money balance, transactions, savings, loans, job assignment, investments, insurance, property, marketplace listings, and similar records. This is entirely fictional in-app currency and activity — it is not real money, and no real financial or payment information is collected.

Locally stored preferences. A few small settings are kept only in your own browser (never sent to our servers beyond what's needed to keep you signed in): your light/dark theme choice, whether the sidebar is collapsed, which notifications or events you've already seen, whether you prefer paying with cash or card in the store and marketplace, other small display choices, a login session marker, and a temporary login lockout timer (see Section 8). When a teacher asks for a password reset email, their username and the recovery email they typed are also kept in that browser so the emailed link can be completed; this is removed once the reset is finished, or you can clear it by clearing your browser's site data. If you tick "remember me" at login, only your username is saved on that device — never your password. Teachers who use the bulk account tool may also have their last-used welcome message template saved in their browser; it doesn't include student passwords. On the Gambling page, your "Reduce effects" choice is also saved in your browser. To keep animations running smoothly, the page briefly checks your device's performance (such as animation speed and basic hardware information your browser reports) and adjusts visual effects to match. This check happens only on your device; nothing from it is saved or sent to us.

Optional device alerts. Students can choose to turn on alerts for the device they're using. If you do, your browser will ask for permission to show notifications, and a small helper script (a service worker) is installed in that browser so alerts can appear on phones and tablets. The alerts are worked out on your device from your own in-app activity while the app is open; this choice is stored only in that browser, and no notification addresses or push tokens are collected or sent to any server. You can turn alerts off at any time in the app or in your browser's settings.

3. How information is used

Information is used only to run the classroom economy: showing balances and transactions, applying jobs/loans/interest, running class-wide events, generating the reports a teacher sees for their own class, and keeping you signed in. A teacher's recovery email is used only to send password reset links to that teacher. We do not use the data for advertising, and we do not use third-party analytics or tracking tools.

4. Who can see your information

  • Your teacher can see account and activity data for students in their own class — this is how they run and grade the simulation.
  • Classmates in the same class can see your display name, your cash balance, and your overall net worth on the class leaderboard, along with anything you post to the marketplace or trade offers. They cannot see your password, login details, or your full private transaction history.
  • Family members you share a link with. A student, or their teacher, can choose to make a read-only "family link" to that student's report card (their display name, class name, teacher's name, job, play-money totals such as balance, savings, investments, loans and net worth, income and spending summaries, and savings goals). Anyone who has that link can view that summary without logging in, but can't change anything. The link is long and random, it's only made if someone chooses to, and it can be turned off at any time from the Reports page — after which it stops working straight away.
  • Other teachers, if you share a class setup. A teacher can choose to share a class's setup (its rules, jobs and similar settings) as a link so another teacher can copy it. The shared template includes the class name and the teacher's name, but not student accounts or student activity. The teacher can stop sharing at any time, and the link is also removed if the class is deleted.
  • The developer may access data as needed to maintain, debug, or support the app, which includes a teacher's recovery email if they have added one.
  • Google stores and processes data on our behalf as the provider of the underlying database and authentication service (Firebase), under Google's own terms. The Firebase software the app runs on is also loaded from Google's servers, so Google receives standard technical information (such as your IP address and browser type) whenever your browser makes those requests. Google also sends the password reset email itself, so a teacher's recovery email address is processed by Google when a reset is requested.

We do not sell data, and we do not share it with advertisers or other third parties.

5. Where information is stored

Account and simulation data is stored using Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Firebase Authentication). Access to a class's data is restricted so that students can only reach their own account and the shared, in-class features described above, and teachers can only reach their own class. Because we use Google's Firebase service, this can mean some data is processed on servers located outside New Zealand. The Privacy Act 2020's rule on sending personal information overseas (Information Privacy Principle 12) allows this where the receiving organisation is subject to privacy laws that provide comparable protection — as is the case with an established provider like Google — or another recognised safeguard applies.

Links to other sites. The app and its About page link to other websites, including a Buy Me a Coffee page for supporting the creator and the creator's other projects. Those sites are run separately and have their own privacy practices, and this policy doesn't cover them. The app doesn't send them any of your account or simulation data.

6. How long we keep information, and your choices

Data is kept for as long as the account or class exists. A teacher can remove an individual student or permanently delete an entire class, which removes the class, its student accounts and any family links. A teacher's own account and recovery email are not removed when a class is deleted. A teacher can remove the recovery email from Teacher Home at any time, or use the "Delete my account" button there, which permanently deletes every class they own (with its student accounts and family links), their recovery email and their login. Classes where they are only a co-teacher are not deleted. A class a teacher has archived (rather than deleted) is kept, and can be reopened, until the teacher deletes it. You — or a parent/guardian, for a student account — can also contact us directly at any time to ask what information a specific account has stored, to correct it, or to have it deleted. We aim to respond to these requests within 20 working days, in line with the Privacy Act.

7. Children's privacy

This app is designed to be used by students in a classroom, under a teacher's supervision. Students don't sign themselves up independently — a teacher creates the class and gives out the class code used to join it, and it's used as a supervised classroom tool rather than a public consumer app. As noted above, we do not collect email addresses, phone numbers, or other direct contact details from students (the optional recovery email is for teachers only), and no advertising or tracking of any kind is used. New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020 doesn't set separate rules specifically for children (unlike some other countries), but it protects everyone's personal information regardless of age, and that's the standard this policy is written to. If you're a parent or guardian with questions about, or wanting to review or remove, a child's account, you can contact your child's teacher, who manages the class, or reach out to us directly using the details in Section 12.

8. Cookies & tracking

This app does not use advertising or tracking cookies, and does not use third-party analytics. It sets one small, strictly functional cookie, called t29_device_lock, and only when someone enters too many wrong passwords. It records when the 30-minute login lockout ends so the lockout still applies on that browser, and it expires automatically when the lockout does. It is not used to identify or track you. All other data kept in your browser is the local, on-device information described in Section 2, used solely to make the app work on your device.

9. Security

We use Firebase Authentication and per-account access rules to help keep your data restricted to the people described above. No online service can guarantee perfect security, but we take reasonable steps to protect your information. If a breach ever occurs that's likely to cause serious harm, we'll notify the Privacy Commissioner and anyone affected as soon as practicable, as required under the Act.

10. New Zealand Privacy Act 2020

This app is built for use in New Zealand classrooms, and this policy is written to be consistent with the Privacy Act 2020. The Act applies to any organisation or individual that holds personal information — including a small, independently-run app like this one, with no minimum size or turnover threshold — and it's what gives you the rights described in Section 6 to ask what information we hold about you and to have it corrected. The contact in Section 12 is the point of contact for privacy questions and requests.

11. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update the "Last updated" date at the top of this page.

12. Contact us

Questions about this policy, or requests about your data, can be sent to Nathan.liu1211@gmail.com.
