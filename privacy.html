Privacy Policy

Version 1.4, last updated 27 November 2026. Next review due by 27 November 2027.

29 World™ is a classroom economy simulator that teachers use to run an in-class currency, bank, jobs, and marketplace for their students. This page explains what information the app collects, how it's used, and who can see it.

1. Who this applies to

Two kinds of accounts use this app: teachers, who create a class and set its rules, and students, who join a specific class using a class code the teacher gives them. Everything below applies to both, except where noted.

2. Information we collect

Creating an account requires a username, display name and password. The app also stores the classroom activity and optional information described below.

Account information. When you create an account, we store the username, display name, and role (teacher or student) you provide, along with the class you belong to. Teachers can also choose a title (such as Ms or Mr) and a last name, which is used so students see their teacher addressed that way. We do not ask for or collect a phone number, date of birth, or home address from anyone, and we never ask students for an email address. Behind the scenes, the login system requires an email-shaped identifier to work, so the app generates a placeholder one from your username automatically, it isn't a working address and nothing is ever sent to it.

Optional teacher recovery email. Teachers can choose to add an email address when they sign up, or later from Teacher Home, so they can get back into their account if they forget their password. It is optional, only teachers can add one, and it can be changed or removed at any time from Teacher Home (removing it also removes the fingerprint described below). The address is kept in a separate record that only that teacher (and the developer, see Section 4) can read; students and classmates cannot see it. To check a username and email pair before sending a reset email, the app also stores a one-way fingerprint (a hash) of the username and email. This fingerprint can be looked up by username without signing in, but it does not contain the email address and the address can't be read out of it, although someone who already knows a username could test guesses of the email against it. When a teacher who has forgotten their password enters their username and recovery email, and the pair matches, Google Firebase sends a one-time sign-in link to that address. Opening the link lets the teacher choose a new password, and from then on that teacher signs in with their recovery email and the new password instead of their username. If the pair doesn't match, no email is sent, and the screen looks the same either way.

Password. Accounts use Google Firebase Authentication, which does not give the app a readable copy of the password. When a teacher creates a batch of student accounts, the passwords are generated in the teacher's browser, shown to the teacher once, and are not saved by this app; it's up to the teacher to hand them out and keep any printed or downloaded list safe.

Failed login attempts. To protect accounts from password guessing, we keep a small counter for each username recording how many wrong passwords have recently been entered and when. After too many wrong attempts the account is locked for 30 minutes. This record contains no IP address or device details, and it resets after a successful login or once the lockout period has passed.

In-app simulation data. As you use the app, it stores the data needed to run the simulation: your play-money balance, transactions, savings, loans, job assignment, investments, insurance, property, marketplace listings, budgets, savings goals, quiz results, payslips, credit-score history, a net-worth figure noted every few days for report cards, your preferred default way to pay (cash or card) in the store and marketplace, the time you last marked notifications as read, and similar records. This is entirely fictional in-app currency and activity, it is not real money, and no real financial or payment information is collected.

Avatars and Comets. Students can design a cartoon avatar by choosing from items built into the app. There is no photo upload and no camera access. We store the look you chose, the items you own, and a Comets balance (Comets are an in-app reward currency that teachers can give out or take back). When a teacher changes a student's Comets, the app also keeps the 20 most recent entries of that log, each with a date, an amount, a short note and the teacher's display name. Avatars and Comets are part of your simulation account record, so they can be seen by the people described in Section 4, and avatars appear next to student names on the teacher's class list.

Online status and last opened. So that a teacher can see who is on the site, the app keeps a small record for each student in a class: when they last opened the app, the last time the app saw them actively using it, and whether they signed out. It updates about every 2 minutes while the page is visible and you have recently clicked, typed, touched or scrolled (and stops after 5 minutes of no activity), and once more when you log out. A student counts as "online" if the last update was within the past 3 minutes. The times are set by our server, and no IP address, location or device details are recorded. Only the teachers of the class can read these records; classmates cannot. A teacher using Student View is not recorded. The class itself also keeps a "last active" date (updated at most every 6 hours when someone in the class uses the app), which is used to archive inactive classes after 3 months.

Daily time limits. If a teacher turns on a daily time limit for a class, the app counts the active time each student spends on the site that day (only while the page is visible and in focus) and saves the running total and its date to the student's account record so the limit can be applied. When the limit is reached, the student sees a lock screen and can ask their teacher for extra time; the request and the teacher's answer are saved too. Nothing is counted for classes that have no limit set.

Requests to your teacher. Some actions need a teacher's approval, such as asking for extra time, changing a side hustle or job, a card or bank account, a marketplace listing or sublet, ticking off a job task, or resetting report-card numbers. When a student sends one, the app saves a short title, a short message (both limited in length) and the date in a record for the class that only that class's teachers can read, where it appears in the teacher's notification bell. Each student has one slot per kind of request, which is replaced or cancelled when the request changes.

Job check-ins and daily questions. If a teacher sets a job to use check-ins, a daily questionnaire or task tick-offs, the student's account record keeps, for each recent day, which job it was for, whether it was completed correctly, when, and how many tries it took. This is used to work out pay for the pay cycle. The log entry does not include the answers a student typed.

CVs. A student's CV draft is stored in a private Firebase record so the student can resume on another device. When a student applies for a job, a copy of that CV is saved with the application, and only that student, the teachers of their class and the developer can open it. The student and developer can access the private draft. Use classroom examples in your CV and leave out private contact details.

Locally stored preferences. A few small settings are kept only in your own browser (never sent to our servers beyond what's needed to keep you signed in): your light/dark theme choice, whether the sidebar is collapsed, which notifications or events you've already seen, which sidebar pages you use most (used only to order the menu), whether you have dismissed the guided tour or other hints, how many Comets you have already been told about, the time of your last online-status update, whether keyboard shortcuts are switched on, other small display choices (a copy of your cash-or-card payment choice may also be kept here), a login session marker, and a temporary login lockout timer (see Section 8). In the teacher's Student View, which student is open is kept only for that browser tab's session. When a teacher asks for a password reset email, their username and the recovery email they typed are also kept in that browser so the emailed link can be completed; this is removed once the reset is finished, or you can clear it by clearing your browser's site data. If you tick "remember me" at login, only your username is saved on that device, never your password. Teachers who use the bulk account tool may also have their last-used welcome message template saved in their browser; it doesn't include student passwords. On the Gambling page, your "Reduce effects" choice is also saved in your browser. To keep animations running smoothly, the page briefly checks your device's performance (such as animation speed and basic hardware information your browser reports) and adjusts visual effects to match. This check happens only on your device; nothing from it is saved or sent to us.

Optional device alerts. Students can choose to turn on alerts for the device they're using. If you do, your browser will ask for permission to show notifications, and the app's service worker (see "Offline use and saved app files" below) is used so alerts can appear on phones and tablets. The alerts are worked out on your device from your own in-app activity while the app is open; this choice is stored only in that browser, and no notification addresses or push tokens are collected or sent to any server. You can turn alerts off at any time in the app or in your browser's settings.

Offline use and saved app files. On every page the app registers a service worker, the same one used for device alerts, that saves a copy of the app's own pages, scripts, styles and Firebase library files in your browser's cache so pages open faster and still load on slow or dropped Wi-Fi. It does not store account, class or simulation data: sign-in and database requests go straight to Google and are not cached. While you are offline a banner tells you that changes cannot be saved. The app can also be installed to a home screen or desktop as a web app. You can remove the saved files at any time by clearing your browser's site data.

Teacher worksheet drafts. Worksheet text, questions, answer keys, layout settings and removed/reset worksheet states are saved to the teacher's account in Cloud Firestore so they can sync across devices. These records are separate from the shared class data: only that teacher account and the developer can access them. The browser keeps a local copy and a queue of unsynced edits for offline work. When upgrading from browser-only worksheets, existing drafts on that device are uploaded to the teacher account; conflicting versions are kept as separate copies. The last selected module and worksheet stay as preferences on each device. Exported worksheet backups are files you control.

Reports, exports and printouts. The app can create files and printouts from data you can already see: a student's report card (as a PDF or to print), a teacher's full class export (a PDF or Markdown file with the class settings, every student's account details, calculated net worth and ratings, and the whole transaction log), and, when a teacher creates student accounts in bulk, a list of the new usernames and one-time passwords (to copy, download as CSV, print as slips, or send using a pre-filled email message). These files are built in your browser and saved to your device; they are not uploaded to or stored by us. The class export leaves out passwords, sign-in identifiers and the private tokens behind family and template links, but otherwise contains student information. The bulk email option only opens the teacher's own email app with the text filled in and no recipient, and the teacher chooses where to send it. Once created, a file is a separate copy under your control, so please store, share and delete it carefully, in line with your school's policies.

If you don't give us information. A username, display name and password are needed to create an account, so without them you can't use 29 World™. Everything else (a teacher's recovery email, an avatar, a CV draft, family links and the other optional features described above) is optional. A teacher who doesn't add a recovery email can't reset their own password by email.

3. How information is used

Information is used only to run the classroom economy: showing balances and transactions, applying jobs/loans/interest, running class-wide events, generating the reports a teacher sees for their own class, and keeping you signed in. Online status, daily time totals, job check-in records and requests are used only to give teachers the classroom tools described in Section 2 (seeing who is online, applying a daily limit the teacher chose, working out pay, and handling approvals). A teacher's recovery email is used only to send password reset links to that teacher. We do not use the data for advertising, and we do not use third-party analytics or tracking tools.

4. Who can see your information

  • Your teacher and any co-teachers can see account and activity data for students in their class, so they can run and grade the simulation. This includes each student's avatar, whether they are online now and when they last opened the app, time used against any daily limit, job check-in records, and the requests they send.
  • Classmates. The screens show classmates your display name, net worth and its breakdown, marketplace listings and trade offers. However, the current database rules also let signed-in members of the same class read classmates' full simulation account records and the shared recent class transaction history, even when those details are not shown on screen. This can include usernames, balances, savings goals, jobs, loans, avatars, Comets, daily time-used totals, job check-in records, activity summaries and transaction notes. Online-status records and requests to teachers are stored separately and cannot be read by classmates. Do not put private or sensitive information in simulation records or notes. Firebase-managed passwords and teachers' separately stored recovery email addresses are not included in these shared records.
  • Family members you share a link with. A student, or their teacher, can choose to make a read-only "family link" to that student's report card (their display name, class name, teacher's name, job, play-money totals such as balance, savings, investments, loans and net worth, income and spending summaries, and savings goals). Anyone who has that link can view that summary without logging in, but can't change anything. The link is long and random, it's only made if someone chooses to, and it can be turned off at any time from the Reports page, after which it stops working straight away.
  • Other teachers, if you share a class setup. A teacher can choose to share a class's setup (its rules, jobs and similar settings) as a link so another teacher can copy it. The shared template includes the class name and the teacher's name, but not student accounts or student activity. The teacher can stop sharing at any time, and the link is also removed if the class is deleted.
  • The developer may access data as needed to maintain, debug, or support the app, which includes a teacher's recovery email if they have added one.
  • Google stores and processes data on our behalf as the provider of the underlying database and authentication service (Firebase), under Google's own terms. The Firebase software the app runs on is also loaded from Google's servers, so Google receives standard technical information (such as your IP address and browser type) whenever your browser makes those requests. Google also sends the password reset email itself, so a teacher's recovery email address is processed by Google when a reset is requested.

We do not sell data, and we do not share it with advertisers or other third parties. The companies that run our infrastructure are listed in Section 5.

5. Where information is stored and who handles it

Account and simulation data is stored using Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore and Firebase Authentication). Access to a class's data is restricted so that students can only reach their own account and the shared, in-class features described above, and teachers can only reach their own class. Because we use Google's Firebase service, some data is processed on servers located outside New Zealand (see "Overseas disclosure" below). The Privacy Act 2020's rule on sending personal information overseas (Information Privacy Principle 12) allows this where the receiving organisation is subject to privacy laws that provide comparable protection, as is the case with an established provider like Google, or another recognised safeguard applies.

Overseas disclosure. Yes, we disclose personal information to overseas companies. Account and simulation data is held by Google in the Google Cloud region Sydney, Australia (australia-southeast1), and Google may also process it in the United States and other countries where Google and its own sub-processors operate. GitHub, which hosts the website's files, is based in the United States and may handle requests through servers in other countries.

Class summaries. Some class details (the class name, teacher and co-teacher usernames, created, last-active and archive dates, and the number of students) are also copied into a summary record used to list a teacher's classes. Only the teachers of that class can read it, and it is removed when the class is deleted.

Links to other sites. The app and its About page link to other websites, including a Buy Me a Coffee page for supporting the creator and the creator's other projects. Those sites are run separately and have their own privacy practices, and this policy doesn't cover them. The app doesn't send them any of your account or simulation data.

6. How long we keep information, and your choices

Data is kept for as long as the account or class exists. Removing an individual student deletes their current simulation account and family link, but entries already saved in the class's transaction history and archived reports remain until the class is deleted. Permanently deleting a class removes its main class record, current student simulation accounts and family links. Student sign-in records in Firebase Authentication and their username mappings are not removed by these teacher controls; contact the developer to request their removal. Previously downloaded exports are separate copies and are not erased by deleting anything in the app.

Online-status records and requests to teachers are kept in separate records for each class, and the app's delete controls (removing a student, or deleting a class) do not currently remove them. They hold usernames, timestamps and short request text. Contact the developer to have them removed.

A teacher's own account and recovery email are not removed when a class is deleted. A teacher can remove the recovery email from Teacher Home at any time, or use "Delete my account" to delete every class they own (with the simulation accounts and family links described above), their recovery email, their own username mapping and their teacher login. If deleting the sign-in record fails after the app data is removed, the app tells the teacher to contact the developer to finish. Classes where they are only a co-teacher are not deleted; their co-teacher membership is removed. An archived class, including one archived automatically after 3 months with no activity, is kept and can be reopened until the teacher deletes it. You, or a parent/guardian for a student, can contact us at any time to ask what information an account has stored, to correct it, or to request deletion of remaining records. Asking to see, correct or delete your information is free. We will reply within 20 working days, in line with the Privacy Act. If a request needs longer (for example, because records have to be removed by hand from Firebase Authentication), we will tell you why within those 20 working days and finish it no later than 3 months after you asked.

Worksheet drafts belong to the teacher account across classes. Deleting a class does not delete those drafts. Deleting the teacher account removes its saved worksheet records, including removed/reset states. Local browser copies and downloaded backup files remain until their browser data or files are cleared.

7. Children's privacy

This app is designed to be used by students in a classroom, under a teacher's supervision. Students don't sign themselves up independently, a teacher creates the class and gives out the class code used to join it, and it's used as a supervised classroom tool rather than a public consumer app. As noted above, we do not collect email addresses, phone numbers, or other direct contact details from students (the optional recovery email is for teachers only), and no advertising or third-party tracking is used. Teachers can, however, see simple activity information about their own students, namely whether they are online, when they last opened the app, and time used against any daily limit, as described in Section 2. New Zealand's Privacy Act 2020 doesn't set separate rules specifically for children (unlike some other countries), but it protects everyone's personal information regardless of age, and that's the standard this policy is written to. If you're a parent or guardian with questions about, or wanting to review or remove, a child's account, you can contact your child's teacher, who manages the class, or reach out to us directly using the details in Section 13.

8. Cookies & tracking

This app does not use advertising or tracking cookies, and does not use third-party analytics. It sets one small, strictly functional cookie, called t29_device_lock, and only when someone enters too many wrong passwords. It records when the 30-minute login lockout ends so the lockout still applies on that browser, and it expires automatically when the lockout does. It is not used to identify or track you. All other data kept in your browser is the local, on-device information described in Section 2, used solely to make the app work on your device. The saved app files and browser storage described in Section 2 live on your device and are not cookies.

9. Security

We use Firebase Authentication and per-account access rules to help keep your data restricted to the people described above. No online service can guarantee perfect security, but we take reasonable steps to protect your information. If a breach ever affects personal information, we will tell the affected teachers and schools as soon as possible, and within 72 hours of confirming it, including what happened and what information was involved. If it is likely to cause serious harm, we will also notify the Privacy Commissioner and the people affected as soon as practicable, as the Act requires, and within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.

10. New Zealand Privacy Act 2020

This app is built for use in New Zealand classrooms, and this policy is written to be consistent with the Privacy Act 2020. The Act applies to any organisation or individual that holds personal information, including a small, independently-run app like this one, with no minimum size or turnover threshold, and it's what gives you the rights described in Section 6 to ask what information we hold about you and to have it corrected. The contact in Section 13 is the point of contact for privacy questions and requests.

12. Privacy complaints

If you think we have breached your privacy, email Nathan.liu1211@gmail.com with what happened, your username, and what you would like us to do. We will:

  • reply within 5 working days to confirm we have your complaint;
  • look into it, and ask you or your teacher for more detail if we need it;
  • aim to give you a written outcome within 20 working days, explaining what we found and what we will do about it.

Students and parents can also ask their teacher to pass a complaint on to us. If you are not happy with how we handle it, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner at privacy.org.nz (freephone 0800 803 909) at any time.
